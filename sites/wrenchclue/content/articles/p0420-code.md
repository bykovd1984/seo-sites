# P0420 Code: What Bank 1 Means and When to Replace the Converter

P0420 means **Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 1)**. The engine computer ran a check on the catalytic converter system and found that its performance fell short. A deteriorated converter is one possible cause, but an exhaust leak, a sensor that responds incorrectly, or a problem with the mixture entering the engine can also affect the result. Before authorizing a converter, ask what evidence points to the converter itself. [Innova's P0420 guide](https://guides.innova.com/codes/p0420) identifies these as separate diagnostic possibilities.

## What Bank 1 tells you about the location

Bank 1 is the group of cylinders that includes cylinder No. 1. An inline engine has just one cylinder bank. On a V-engine, Bank 1 could be on either side as you stand in front of the car. Use a cylinder layout for the exact engine; a driver's-side or passenger-side rule can send you to the wrong exhaust pipe.

Bank and sensor numbers answer different questions. The bank number tells the technician which cylinders feed the exhaust being monitored. A sensor number identifies a sensor position. **Bank 1 does not mean Sensor 1**, and P0420 doesn't select an oxygen sensor for replacement. P0430 is the corresponding catalyst-efficiency code for Bank 2. [Ford's gasoline OBD system summary](https://www.fordservicecontent.com/Ford_Content/catalog/motorcraft/OBD_Operation_Summary_to_Gasoline_MY_2024_2025.pdf) lists the two banks separately.

The basic meaning applies whether the badge says Toyota, Honda, Ford, or Chevrolet. What you need from the year and engine is the exhaust layout, the type and location of the sensors, and the manufacturer's procedure for evaluating their readings.

## What the car feels like matters

You may notice only a steady check engine light. The converter treats exhaust after combustion, so the car can accelerate normally even when the efficiency check fails. If the light is steady and the engine runs normally, save the scan results and arrange diagnosis. There's no need to make the car run poorly to demonstrate the complaint.

A marked loss of power raises a different question: can exhaust flow out of the engine freely? A converter can lose its ability to treat exhaust without plugging the pipe. Conversely, a broken or shifted internal core can obstruct flow. [Walker's converter FAQ](https://www.walkerexhaust.com/support/tech-tips/common-catalytic-converter-q-and-a.html) explains how internal damage can cause a restriction. If loss of power is part of your complaint, ask whether the shop tested for restriction as well as efficiency.

If the [check engine light is flashing](flashing-check-engine-light.md) and the engine is shaking, get safely out of traffic, shut it off, and arrange a tow. Report the flashing and shaking first, even if P0420 is the only code you remember. An active misfire changes the immediate decision and can damage the catalyst.

## How a shop separates four possible causes

Before clearing anything, save all codes, their status, and any available freeze-frame data: the snapshot of engine conditions recorded when a fault was detected. Include recent exhaust work and any earlier misfire or fuel-mixture repair. A scan made after the memory was erased can't tell the technician everything that was present beforehand.

| Finding | What the shop needs to establish |
|---|---|
| A misfire or mixture code accompanies P0420 | Is the engine still sending abnormal exhaust into the converter? Diagnose the engine fault before evaluating the converter's remaining performance. |
| There's a leak near the monitored exhaust section | Could outside air be affecting a sensor reading? Locate and repair the relevant leak, then reassess. |
| A sensor reading looks unusual | Does the sensor respond as expected for this engine, and are its wiring and connector sound? A reading can reflect a real exhaust condition. |
| The engine runs correctly, the exhaust is sealed, and the sensors pass their checks | Does the Bank 1 catalyst still fail the prescribed efficiency test? That result gives a converter recommendation a factual basis. |

The distinction matters when looking at a graph from a scan tool. The computer uses information from before and after the converter to assess its behavior. Two traces that look alike in a screenshot may warrant investigation, but the technician also needs to know what the sensors measure and what the engine was doing. Ask for the interpretation under the vehicle's test conditions, rather than trying to match your graph to one from a different engine.

## Choosing a repair that lasts

If a leak explains the failed check, the repair may be at an exhaust joint rather than inside the converter. If testing identifies a damaged sensor circuit, the estimate should say whether the fault is in the sensor, connector, or wiring. In either case, find out how the shop will confirm that the efficiency check completes successfully after the repair. A light that goes out immediately after a reset doesn't answer that question.

When the converter itself has deteriorated, ask one more question: **what was the engine sending through it?** Misfire or rich operation can overheat a catalyst; oil or coolant entering the exhaust can contaminate it. A replacement exposed to the same conditions can fail again. [Walker discusses these causes of repeat converter failure](https://www.walkerexhaust.com/support/tech-tips/common-catalytic-converter-q-and-a.html). Tell the shop if you've been adding oil or coolant between services. That history is useful even if you don't know where the fluid went.

For a replacement estimate, have the shop identify the Bank 1 assembly, match the part to the vehicle's emissions label, and separate the part, installation labor, and any underlying engine repair. Two estimates are hard to compare if one includes correcting a misfire and the other only replaces the converter.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does P0420 mean my converter is clogged?** No. The code concerns exhaust treatment. If the car also struggles to make power, ask for a separate assessment of exhaust flow.

**Should I replace the downstream oxygen sensor first because it's cheaper?** Only if testing supports it. A functioning downstream sensor may be the component reporting poor catalyst performance. Replacing it would leave the cause in place.

**Why did P0420 return after a new converter?** Start with the original work order. Confirm the correct assembly was installed on the affected bank, check the installation for leaks, and find out whether any mixture or misfire problem was resolved. The returning code alone can't distinguish among those possibilities.
