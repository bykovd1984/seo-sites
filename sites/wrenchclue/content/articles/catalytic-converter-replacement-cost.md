# Catalytic Converter Replacement Cost and How to Compare Quotes

Catalytic converter replacement has a broad US estimate of **$2,280–$2,480**, with parts alone estimated at **$2,044–$2,133**. RepairPal updated those figures September 3, 2026, and they were checked November 8, 2026. The first detail to look for on a local quote is which converter the shop intends to replace. A vehicle can have more than one, and the correct part depends on its position, the engine, and the vehicle's emissions certification. [RepairPal catalytic converter replacement estimate](https://repairpal.com/estimator/catalytic-converter-replacement-cost)

## Where the money goes

| Estimate item | Published US range | What to look for on the written estimate |
|---|---:|---|
| Converter parts | $2,044–$2,133 | Part number, quantity, converter position, and included exhaust components |
| Installation labor | $236–$347 | Access, removal, fitting, and any additional exhaust work |
| Replacement total | $2,280–$2,480 | Which charges are included and what could still change |

RepairPal's ranges exclude tax, fees, adjustments for your location, and related repairs. They provide context for the scale of the purchase. They do not establish the installed price of every converter on a particular car. If a quote lists two assemblies, ask for the price and reason for replacing each before comparing the bottom line with a national figure. [RepairPal estimate details](https://repairpal.com/estimator/catalytic-converter-replacement-cost)

The part is expensive in part because the active catalyst inside the housing treats exhaust gases. The replacement can also include a shaped pipe section or an exhaust manifold, depending on the design. When comparing an online part with a shop's price, check the diagram. A photo of a small converter body and a drawing of a manifold assembly with a converter built in may represent quite different purchases.

## Match the bank, position, and installation work

On an engine with two cylinder banks, the bank identifies the side of the engine the exhaust comes from. The position tells you where the converter sits along that exhaust path. Have the shop identify both where applicable. A quote that says only *catalytic converter* leaves room for one shop to price a unit near the engine while another prices one farther along the exhaust.

Location affects access. Removing a converter joined to a manifold involves different work from replacing an accessible pipe assembly under the vehicle. Ask how the new assembly connects, which gaskets and fasteners are included, and whether damaged adjoining pipe would require a separate authorization. If an oxygen sensor appears on the parts list, ask whether the shop found a sensor fault or physical damage, or simply needs to remove and reinstall the existing sensor.

The distinction matters after theft as well. A replacement quote may cover the converter and the pipe or wiring damaged when it was taken. Ask the estimator to separate that damage from the basic converter installation. It gives you a clear account of why the vehicle in front of the shop needs more work than the price in a parts catalog suggests.

## Choosing a legal replacement for your vehicle

A vehicle manufacturer's original-equipment assembly and an aftermarket assembly are worth comparing when both are available and appropriate. Give the installer the vehicle year, make, model, engine, and the emissions information from its label. Physical dimensions alone are insufficient to select the converter. Have the shop identify the exact part it will install and confirm the requirements where you register and use the vehicle. [Walker converter fitment guidance](https://www.walkerexhaust.com/support/tech-tips/common-catalytic-converter-q-and-a.html)

**In California**, an aftermarket catalytic converter sold or installed for the vehicle needs an applicable California Air Resources Board exemption Executive Order, or EO. Approval applies to specified vehicle applications. Ask the installer to match the EO listing to your model year, engine size, and test group or engine family. Two cars with the same model name can require different checks against that listing. [California Air Resources Board aftermarket converter guidance](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/aftermarket-catalytic-converters)

If you're shopping for the least expensive repair, give each shop the same vehicle information and ask for its lowest installed price for a qualifying part. An inexpensive listing becomes a comparable offer once you know the application, included hardware, and cost to fit it. There is no fixed aftermarket discount you can apply to the national estimate.

## Two checks before authorizing replacement

**Find out what caused the failure.** A converter can suffer heat damage from a misfire or excessively rich operation, and oil or coolant can contaminate it. If the original fault continues, the replacement may suffer the same damage. An efficiency code alone also leaves open whether the exhaust is physically restricted. Ask what evidence supports the diagnosis and whether an engine repair needs its own line on the estimate. [Walker catalytic converter FAQ](https://www.walkerexhaust.com/support/tech-tips/common-catalytic-converter-q-and-a.html) · [P0420 diagnosis and repair options](p0420-code.md)

**Check whether a defect may qualify for warranty coverage.** EPA's federal warranty fact sheet describes coverage for defects in specified major emissions components, including catalytic converters, on qualifying 1995-and-newer light-duty cars and trucks for **8 years or 80,000 miles, whichever comes first**. Before paying, contact the manufacturer or dealer with the VIN, mileage, vehicle age, and diagnosis. Ask them to check the warranty booklet and eligibility for this failure. Theft and collision damage are different from a component defect, and California coverage can differ. [EPA emissions warranty fact sheet](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P100AU05.TXT)

## Frequently asked questions

**Does one failed converter mean the car needs them all?** Ask which unit failed and how the technician identified it. Also ask how many converters are physically part of the replacement assembly. Buying one assembly and authorizing replacement of every separate converter on the vehicle are different decisions.

**Can a less expensive repair resolve a converter-related complaint?** Yes, if testing finds that the complaint originates elsewhere or that only an adjoining exhaust section needs repair. Ask for the finding that makes converter replacement necessary, particularly if the initial recommendation came from a code scan alone. [P0420 diagnostic guide](p0420-code.md)

**Why might two shops quote similar parts prices but different labor?** Compare what each plans to remove, fit, and reuse. One quote may include restoring damaged pipe or hardware that the other leaves open pending inspection. Ask both shops for the total assuming the same work.

**What documents should I keep?** Keep the diagnosis, itemized invoice, fitted part number, and any written warranty terms. For a California aftermarket installation, retain the applicable EO information with the vehicle records so the installed application can be identified later. [CARB aftermarket converter guidance](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/aftermarket-catalytic-converters)
