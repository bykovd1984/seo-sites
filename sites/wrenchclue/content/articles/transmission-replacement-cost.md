# How Much Does It Cost to Repair, Rebuild, or Replace a Transmission?

**RepairPal estimates $6,238–$6,764 for transmission replacement in the US**, including about $5,110 for parts and $1,127–$1,654 for labor. A targeted repair and a rebuild of your existing transmission require their own estimates. A repair price depends on which fault the shop can isolate. A rebuild price also depends on what the rebuilder finds inside your unit. [RepairPal transmission replacement estimate](https://repairpal.com/estimator/transmission-replacement-cost)

Before committing to removal, ask the shop to put its diagnosis and proposed remedy on separate lines. “Delayed engagement when shifting into Drive” describes what the car does. An explanation of the fault, the evidence for it, and what a particular repair would correct gives you something to use when deciding whether to spend several thousand dollars.

## What the replacement benchmark tells you

Parts account for most of RepairPal's replacement estimate. That makes the identity of the quoted transmission especially important. One shop may have priced a used assembly and another a remanufactured one. Their labor quotes might be close even when their totals are far apart. Ask for the source and identification number of the proposed unit alongside the installation charge.

RepairPal's figures were updated September 3, 2026, and exclude taxes, fees, your specific location, and possible related repairs. The estimate supplies no separate price for a factory-new transmission, a CVT, or a particular make and model. To budget for your own car, get a written installed total that identifies the transmission and includes any work the installer requires to put it into service. ## Comparing a limited repair, a rebuild, and an exchange

| Option | What you are buying | Question that helps settle the price |
|---|---|---|
| Targeted repair | Correction of an identified fault in the existing transmission or its controls | What finding makes this repair sufficient? |
| Rebuild your unit | Disassembly and inspection of your transmission, followed by the agreed internal work and reassembly | Which parts are included, and which findings could change the total? |
| Used replacement | A previously operated transmission from another vehicle | What is known about its identity, mileage, condition, and return terms? |
| Remanufactured replacement | An exchange assembly processed to the supplier's stated standard | What does the supplier replace, update, and test? |
| New replacement | A newly manufactured assembly, if available for the application | Can the supplier confirm the unit is new and give a delivery date? |

For a limited repair, read the diagnostic explanation first. If the shop believes the problem is confined to one component, ask how it evaluated the possibility of more extensive damage. For a rebuild, the important financial decision can come **before teardown**. Find out what you owe for removal and inspection, what the quoted rebuild includes, and what it would cost to stop or switch to an exchange unit after inspection.

“Remanufactured” deserves a closer look on a parts line. In its 2018 US catalog, ZF described taking units apart, inspecting cleaned components, renewing parts outside specification, applying engineering updates, and testing the assembled transmission under operating conditions. That describes one supplier's process. Ask today's seller for its own build standard and written terms for the specific unit on offer. [ZF remanufactured transmission catalog](https://aftermarket.zf.com/media/us/aapex/zf-parts/full_zf_remantrans_2018catalog_2.pdf)

## Matching the transmission and accounting for installation labor

Identify whether the vehicle has a conventional automatic, continuously variable transmission (CVT), dual-clutch transmission, or manual. Then have the installer confirm the exact application using the VIN and the transmission identification or catalog number as appropriate. A listing with the right model name and a photo of a similar case still needs that fitment check. ZF's application listings illustrate how specific the identification can be. [ZF catalog](https://aftermarket.zf.com/media/us/aapex/zf-parts/full_zf_remantrans_2018catalog_2.pdf)

On the labor side, look for removal of the existing unit and installation of the replacement. Ask whether the total includes the specified fluid, any programming or setup the vehicle requires, and verification of operation. If a supplier charges a refundable deposit for the old transmission, have the shop show the amount due up front, who returns the old unit, and any conditions for receiving the credit.

One question is particularly helpful with a used or exchange unit. If the supplier accepts that the assembly is defective after installation, who pays to take it back out and install the next one? Get the answer from the installer and read the supplier's written terms. The price of the part alone tells you little about that potential expense.

## Repair time and the decision to keep the car

For a rebuild, ask when the shop can remove and inspect your transmission, when it expects to give you a final price, and whether it can confirm parts availability at that point. For a replacement, ask whether the correct assembly is physically available and when the shop can start installation. A unit listed in a catalog and a unit reserved for your vehicle can lead to very different pickup dates.

If you're deciding whether the car is worth repairing, compare the installed total and the cost of transportation during the repair with the cost of obtaining another suitable vehicle. Include other significant work your car already needs. A sound car you plan to keep presents a different decision from one you were about to sell. In the latter case, compare realistic offers for the car as it sits with its likely value after repair before authorizing teardown.

## Questions to settle before authorizing transmission work

**Is rebuilding always cheaper than replacing?** Get both estimates for your exact transmission. Internal damage can change a rebuild quote, while the availability and condition of an exchange unit can change a replacement quote. Ask which price the shop can commit to today.

**Does “new transmission” on a verbal estimate mean factory-new?** Have the advisor identify it in writing as new, remanufactured, or used. Also ask whether the price you heard was for the assembly alone or for the car ready to pick up.

**How do I compare two very different labor charges?** Check which tasks each shop includes. A quote covering removal, installation, fluid, applicable setup, and final checks covers more than a line that lists installation alone. Ask for any remaining charges before comparing totals.

**Can I drive it to get another opinion?** If the transmission loses drive unexpectedly or the car cannot engage a gear reliably enough to move safely in traffic, arrange transport. You can send the written findings and transmission identification to another shop before moving the vehicle.
