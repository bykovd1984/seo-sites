# Brake Pad and Rotor Replacement Costs for One Axle or All Four Wheels

RepairPal estimates **$335–$394 for brake pad replacement** and, in a separate estimate, **$574–$698 for brake rotor replacement**. Neither figure establishes the installed price for pads and rotors together on your front axle, rear axle, or all four wheels. For that price, ask the shop to identify the wheels it will service, the parts it will install, and the labor it will charge for the combined job. ## Front brakes, rear brakes, and what “all four” buys

Pads are ordinarily replaced on both sides of the axle being serviced. A front pad job therefore covers the left and right front brakes. It tells you little about what the rear brakes need. If a quote says only “brake pads,” have the service advisor write in the axle before you compare the total with another shop's offer. [RepairPal pad replacement](https://repairpal.com/estimator/brake-pad-replacement-cost)

The wording matters even on a four-wheel estimate. A shop might recommend pads on both axles and new rotors only on the front. Another might quote pads and rotors at every wheel. Both advisors could reasonably describe their proposals as work on “all four brakes,” yet the second estimate includes another pair of rotors. Ask for separate front and rear lines so you can see the difference.

Give each shop the year, make, model, and trim, and have the VIN available if the parts catalog shows more than one brake package. Then ask which pad set and rotor part numbers the shop priced. That makes a comparison possible when one estimate names a specific product and another simply says “premium brakes.” Use the same approach for a rear-only job. The front estimate is a poor basis for guessing the rear price.

## When the rotors belong on the estimate

On a disc brake, the pads press against the faces of the rotor to slow the wheel. A pads-only estimate makes sense when the existing rotors are suitable for continued service. Before approving new rotors, ask what the technician found on each side. A measurement answers a wear question. A description or photo can help explain a surface defect.

For thickness, ask for the **measured value and the applicable minimum**. A figure written on an inspection sheet means little without the limit for that particular disc. Brembo identifies the minimum with the `MIN TH` marking and recommends replacing both discs on the axle if even one is below its wear limit. That explains why an advisor might show you a measurement from one front rotor and quote two front rotors. [Brembo rotor thickness guidance](https://asia.bremboparts.com/europe/en/support/car/maintenance/minimum-brake-disc-thickness-212337)

If the shop offers to resurface the existing rotors, ask whether their condition permits machining and whether the finished thickness will meet the applicable service specification. Have the advisor price that work explicitly. It gives you a concrete choice between servicing the existing pair and buying a replacement pair when both options are suitable for your vehicle.

## Labor cost and time at the shop

Ask for one installed price for the exact parts you approved. The estimate should show how much goes toward the pad set, each rotor, any additional hardware, and labor. Check the bottom of the page for taxes and fees before comparing it with a price given over the phone. If the shop also recommends a caliper, request a separate finding and price for that repair so you can still see the cost of the pads and rotors.

For scheduling, ask when inspection will finish and when the car will be ready **if you authorize the proposed work**. Billed labor and elapsed time answer different questions. The car may spend part of the appointment waiting for your decision or for the correct rotors to arrive. If you need it back that afternoon, the most useful information is whether the shop has confirmed the brake package, has the parts on hand, and has reserved time to install them.

## Common brake estimate questions

**Can I double a front brake quote to budget for all four wheels?** Get a rear estimate for the same vehicle. The rear axle can require different parts and a different scope of work. Separate axle totals also let you see which work the inspection supports now.

**Is a pads-only phone quote a firm price?** Ask what the shop assumes about rotor condition. If the price depends on reusing both rotors, agree on a call after inspection so you can review the measurements and revised total before authorizing more work.

**What should I ask if one shop is much cheaper?** Read the two parts lists side by side. Confirm the axle, rotor quantity, pad product, installation labor, and fees. If those match, you have a meaningful price difference to discuss with the advisors.

**Should I keep shopping if braking performance has changed?** If the pedal suddenly sinks or the car is difficult to stop, pull over safely and arrange a tow. Tell the shop about the change in pedal feel when you call so it can plan a brake-system inspection.
