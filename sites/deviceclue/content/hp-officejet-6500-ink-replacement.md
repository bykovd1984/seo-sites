# How to replace ink cartridges in an HP Officejet 6500

For the **Officejet 6500 E709 and Officejet 6500A E710**, turn the printer on, open the cartridge access door and wait for the carriage to stop. Release the individual cartridge by its front tab, then install the compatible replacement in the matching color-and-shape slot. Keep the printhead latch down.

Check the complete product name first. HP also sells ENVY and DeskJet printers carrying the number 6500; those are different families. This procedure does not apply merely because a printer has “6500” on its label.

## Prepare the replacement before opening the printer

Match the replacement to the product and region shown on the printer's label or HP supply information. Have it ready before removing the installed cartridge. Do not leave an empty slot while you go shopping for ink.

Keep the new packaging nearby until the printer accepts the cartridge. It provides a useful reference for its exact type if you need to check compatibility or report a problem.

## Replace an individual cartridge

The [Officejet 6500 E709 guide](https://www.hp.com/ctg/Manual/c01669504.pdf) gives this sequence:

1. With the printer powered on, open the cartridge door and wait for all carriage movement to stop.
2. Press the front tab of the cartridge you are replacing and lift it from its slot.
3. Remove the new cartridge's plastic wrapping using the orange pull-tab, then remove its orange protective cap.
4. Match the cartridge's color and shaped symbol to the empty slot. Insert it until it clicks into place.
5. Replace any other required cartridge the same way, then close the door.

Do not lift the carriage's printhead latch for this operation. Cartridge replacement and printhead removal are different jobs.

## What changes for the 6500A

The [Officejet 6500A E710 instructions](https://www.hp.com/ctg/Manual/c02519721.pdf) use the same basic individual-cartridge process. They also specify removing the packaging tab completely so that the vent channel is exposed. Follow the supplied cartridge's packaging instructions and do not cut, drill or enlarge an opening.

A cartridge that does not seat should be checked for correct type, slot and remaining packaging before applying pressure. Do not force the latch or carriage into position.

## Check the result

Read the display after closing the door. Make a small test print only after the printer is ready. Note whether the problem is an explicit cartridge error, a missing color, streaking or a failure to send the print job; those observations lead to different checks.

For example, a cartridge installation message is a reason to revisit the cartridge and slot. A job that never reaches the printer needs a queue or connection investigation. Replacing another color does not test that connection.

## Low ink and poor print quality

HP describes ink-level alerts as estimates for planning replacement. For the E710, poor print quality can also involve the printhead, and cleaning consumes ink. A low warning alone does not prove a cartridge must be discarded immediately.

If the correct cartridge is installed and an error remains, record its exact wording and the cartridge identity. Use the model-specific troubleshooting path rather than repeatedly removing every cartridge. Keep the cartridges and printhead installed when the printer is left unused, following the model's shutdown instructions.
