# What is a printer bypass tray, and when should you use it?

A **bypass tray** is a separate paper feed you can use for a job while the main drawer holds your everyday paper. On the **Sharp BP-70C65**, for example, you can print a few envelopes, a sheet of labels, or a page on a different size of plain paper. The tray takes a stack, but its capacity depends on what you load. Sharp lists up to 100 sheets of ordinary paper and up to 20 envelopes. [Sharp BP-70C65 bypass tray guide](https://global.sharp/restricted/print/manuals/4/bp70c65/us/contents_01-02_005.html)

## Which side faces up on the Sharp BP-70C65?

For ordinary paper, put the side you want to print on **face down**. Slide the leading edge in gently until it stops, keep the stack below the fill line, and bring the guides in until they just touch both edges. Loose guides can let the sheet feed at an angle. [Sharp’s loading instructions](https://global.sharp/restricted/print/manuals/4/bp70c65/us/contents_01-02_005.html)

Two cases are easy to miss. For paper registered as **Letter Head** or **Pre-Printed**, Sharp specifies **face up**. If the printer’s **Disabling of Duplex** setting is enabled, those papers go face down. Envelopes go **address side down**, with the flap positioned as shown for that envelope size. [Sharp’s orientation diagrams and letterhead exception](https://global.sharp/restricted/print/manuals/4/bp70c65/us/contents_01-02_005.html)

## How to print an envelope from Windows

1. Load the envelope. On the printer’s home screen, open **Tray Settings**, select the bypass tray, and set its size and paper type to match the envelope. [Sharp’s tray setup guidance](https://global.sharp/restricted/print/manuals/4/bp70c65/us/contents_10-01_003.html)
2. Open the Sharp driver’s print preferences and select **Paper Source**. Under **Output Size**, choose the envelope size. The driver sets **Paper Type** to **Envelope**.
3. Under **Paper Tray**, choose **Bypass Tray**. Print one envelope first to check the address orientation before sending the rest. [Sharp’s Windows envelope instructions](https://global.sharp/restricted/print/manuals/4/bp70c65/us/contents_04-02_001.html)

## Why does the printer ask for the bypass tray?

Look at **Paper Tray**, **Output Size**, and **Paper Type** in the driver. A leftover envelope setting can affect the next document. If the job belongs on the ordinary paper in the drawer, choose its size and type, set **Paper Tray** to **Auto Select**, then send the corrected job. The driver’s **Tray Status** button shows what the machine reports in each tray. [Sharp’s Windows paper selection guide](https://global.sharp/restricted/print/manuals/4/bp70c65/us/contents_04-02_001.html)

## Why does the job wait when the bypass tray is loaded?

Compare the driver settings with **Tray Settings** on the machine. For example, an envelope can be sitting in the tray while the printer still identifies the stock as plain paper. With bypass paper-type checking enabled, a job requesting an envelope can wait even if its dimensions match. Register the envelope’s actual size and type on the printer, then check that the driver requests the same stock. [Sharp’s bypass tray matching rules](https://global.sharp/restricted/print/manuals/4/bp70c65/us/contents_04-02_001.html)
