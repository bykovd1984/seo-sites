## Where can I find a Millermatic 350P settings chart?

Use the chart and instructions for the **exact Millermatic 350P configuration and revision**, then select the row for the welding process, wire material and diameter, shielding gas and workpiece thickness. There is no single safe voltage and wire-speed pair for every job. The separately documented **350P Aluminum** is a different configuration, so do not copy its chart to a standard 350P without confirming compatibility.

Miller’s 350P specification identifies conventional MIG and pulsed MIG, with different programmed wire/material options. The panel labels include **VOLTS / ARC LENGTH** and **WIRE SPEED / AMPS**; which value is meaningful depends on the selected mode. A setting displayed for pulse arc length is not simply a voltage value.

## A specific chart example: steel with 0.035-inch wire

One Miller OM-1327 MIG chart for the 350/350P gives the following starting references for **steel**, **0.035-inch wire** and **100% CO₂ shielding gas**. Its table explicitly refers to **horizontal T fillet welds**. The two numbers are voltage and wire-feed speed in inches per minute (IPM):

| Material thickness | Voltage | Wire speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/4 in | 21.5 V | 280 IPM |
| 3/16 in | 20.5 V | 240 IPM |
| 1/8 in | 20.0 V | 210 IPM |

These are conditional starting references from a particular edition, not tested results or settings for other gases, wire sizes, processes or joints. Miller says settings may need modification for another joint or position. The [manufacturer-authored OM-1327 chart](https://manualsdump.com/en/manuals/miller_electric-350_350p-millermatic_350/280620/32) is available as a hosted copy; compare it with the chart and revision supplied with your own machine. Consult the applicable qualified procedure before safety-critical work.

## Match the chart to the job

| Check first | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Machine model and revision | A 350P and a 350P Aluminum do not automatically share the same chart. |
| MIG or pulsed MIG | Controls and displayed values differ by process. |
| Wire material and diameter | The machine’s suitable wire ranges and pulse programs depend on the wire. |
| Shielding gas and material thickness | A chart row assumes a particular combination; other jobs need different values. |
| Gun, drive rolls and installed equipment | The selected wire and feeder components must be compatible. |

For the 350P specification sheet, Miller lists solid-steel wire of 0.023–0.045 in and aluminium wire of 0.035–0.047 in. These are **wire-capacity ranges**, not suggested settings for a weld, and do not mean every gun or program supports every combination.

## MIG versus pulse: which setting do I read?

In conventional MIG, a manufacturer chart may specify wire-feed speed and voltage for a particular combination. In pulsed MIG, the 350P uses programmed pulse settings and the display can refer to arc length instead. Read the headings and units on the correct chart rather than transferring numbers between modes. Miller notes that joint geometry and welding position, in addition to metal, thickness, gas and wire, influence the appropriate parameters.

For structural or safety-critical work, follow the applicable qualified welding procedure and inspection requirements; a general chart is a starting reference, not proof of an acceptable weld. Use appropriate eye and skin protection, ventilation and equipment grounding, and follow the manufacturer’s safety instructions.

## Why is my result different from a chart?

A chart is conditional. Recheck the process, material, wire diameter, gas, machine revision and gun before changing settings. Do not treat a value from a forum or another model as a universal fix. If the machine does not feed consistently, the programmed option is unavailable, or the equipment behaves abnormally, use the model-specific troubleshooting information or ask a qualified technician.
