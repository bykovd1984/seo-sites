# Samsung POWERbot not charging: dock and battery checks

If a Samsung POWERbot will not charge, check **the bottom emergency switch, power to the dock and contact alignment** before assuming the battery has failed. A robot that cannot find its dock and one that will not charge while correctly seated on it need different checks.

These steps concern POWERbot models covered by Samsung's cited support information. Jet Bot, POWERbot-E and other Samsung robot families can have different switches, docks and displays. Use the complete model number to confirm the charging instructions.

## Separate a docking problem from a charging problem

Place the robot on its powered, compatible dock according to the model instructions and watch the display. Record what happens rather than judging only by whether it starts cleaning.

| Observation | Next focus |
| --- | --- |
| Charges when placed on the dock | Dock location and automatic return |
| No charging indication while seated | Switch, dock power and contacts |
| Lo appears | Low-battery charging procedure |
| An information code remains | Model-specific code explanation |

For a displayed wheel or bumper warning, use the separate [POWERbot error-code guide](/guides/samsung-powerbot-c03-c05-errors). A cleaning obstruction and an inability to receive charge are different symptoms.

## Check the switch and power

[Samsung's POWERbot charging guidance](https://www.samsung.com/my/support/home-appliances/why-is-my-powerbot-not-working-or-unable-to-charge-properly/) says the emergency switch underneath must be on for the covered models to charge. Check that the dock's adapter is connected and the outlet has power. Use the supply intended for that model.

Inspect charging contacts for visible dirt. With the robot switched off and the dock unplugged for cleaning, wipe contacts with a dry cloth; then restore power and reseat the robot. Do not scrape contacts with metal or open the battery pack.

Stop if the battery is swollen, leaking or unusually hot, or if the adapter is damaged. Repeated attempts to charge a damaged unit are not a useful diagnostic test.

## Align the robot and read its charging display

[Samsung's stopped-cleaning instructions](https://www.samsung.com/za/support/home-appliances/why-has-the-powerbot-stopped-during-cleaning/) show manual docking for a low-battery indication. The robot's contacts must meet the dock contacts. The documented displays differ: some use a blinking battery indication, while another example shows progress bars followed by FULL.

Read the indicator specified for your model. A dock light by itself does not show that the robot is charging. If a deeply discharged unit initially remains unresponsive, Samsung's charging article describes allowing 10–20 minutes before the display can be activated; that is not the full charging time.

## If manual charging works

Check whether the route to the dock is obstructed and whether the dock has been moved. Samsung's placement example calls for a flat, open location with approximately 0.5 m clear at each side and 1 m in front. Follow your own model's clearance requirements if they differ.

Start a cleaning run from the dock and observe whether it returns. This test addresses navigation; it does not measure battery capacity.

## If it still will not charge

Give support the robot and dock model numbers, displayed status and the results of manual docking. Explain whether the robot never charges or charges but runs only briefly. Do not jump-start the pack, bridge its terminals or substitute a charger with an unverified rating.
