# What Canon Auto Lighting Optimizer changes on the EOS R6 Mark II

**Auto Lighting Optimizer** adjusts the brightness and contrast of the finished picture. On the **EOS R6 Mark II**, it can help when a face looks dark against a bright background, or when the picture looks flat. The same adjustment can work against a silhouette if you want the foreground to stay dark. [Canon’s EOS R6 Mark II Auto Lighting Optimizer guide](https://cam.start.canon/en/C012/manual/html/UG-04_Shooting-1_0130.html)

## Which setting should you use?

Open the **Shooting** menu and select **Auto Lighting Optimizer**. The R6 Mark II offers **Disable**, **Low**, **Standard**, and **High**. For a backlit portrait, try **Standard** and check the face. If the shadows look too bright, try **Low**. Canon notes that correction can make noise more visible or change how fine detail looks under some shooting conditions. [Canon’s correction settings](https://cam.start.canon/en/C012/manual/html/UG-04_Shooting-1_0130.html) · [Canon’s list of correction levels](https://cam.start.canon/en/C012/manual/html/UG-10_Reference_0060.html)

## Why does a photo still look bright after negative exposure compensation?

For a deliberately dark photo, choose **Disable**. This is worth checking if you already dialed in negative exposure compensation and the image still looks brighter than you wanted. Canon documents the same interaction when you reduce **flash exposure compensation**. [Canon’s EOS R6 Mark II exposure troubleshooting](https://cam.start.canon/en/C012/manual/html/UG-10_Reference_0060.html)

## How do you enable the optimizer in manual or bulb mode?

If the option is unavailable in **M** (manual exposure) or **B** (bulb), open the correction setting and press **INFO**. Clear **Disabled in M or B modes** to make the adjustment available in those modes. [Canon’s instructions for M and B modes](https://cam.start.canon/en/C012/manual/html/UG-04_Shooting-1_0130.html)

## Can you change it after taking the photo?

If you have the **RAW or C-RAW** file, you can compare two versions of exactly the same exposure. On the camera, open **Playback → RAW processing (RAW/DPRAW)**, choose the shot, and select **Set up processing → JPEG**. Save one JPEG with the optimizer disabled and another with it enabled. Compare the shadow side of the face and any dark, textured areas. The camera keeps the original RAW file for another pass. [Canon’s in-camera RAW processing instructions](https://cam.start.canon/en/C012/manual/html/UG-06_Playback_0190.html)

In **Canon Digital Photo Professional**, open the RAW file and look under **Auto Lighting Optimizer** in the **Basic Adjustment** tool palette. It starts with the setting used in the camera. Adjust the level or clear the checkbox to compare the result. [Canon’s Digital Photo Professional tool guide](https://cam.start.canon/en/S002/manual/html/UG-04_EditImage_0040.html)
