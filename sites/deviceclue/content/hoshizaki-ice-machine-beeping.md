## Count the repeating pattern

Listen for a complete sequence, then the pause before it repeats. Three beeps followed by a pause is a different observation from an occasional sound or a continuous tone. Record a short clip if you can do so without opening service covers.

Write down the model number and when the alarm appeared: at startup, while making ice, or after a period of normal operation. The examples below are drawn from Hoshizaki’s KMD-450MAH / KMD-450MWH service documentation. They are not a complete chart for flakers, dispensers or every later control board.

## What one, two and three beeps can mean

| Repeating pattern | Meaning in the documented system | What it does not identify |
|---|---|---|
| One beep | High-temperature safety protection | A particular failed component |
| Two beeps | Repeated harvest cycles exceeding the allowed time | A universal water-valve diagnosis |
| Three beeps | Repeated freeze cycles exceeding the allowed time | A guaranteed low-refrigerant condition |

The long-harvest and long-freeze protections operate after the documented condition occurs on two consecutive cycles. The freeze timeout is determined by the model and control settings. Treat a generic “always 60 minutes” explanation cautiously; the model documentation is the authority.

## What to record before requesting service

Give the technician useful observations rather than a guessed part name. A brief record can include:

- The complete model and serial number.
- The number of beeps in each repeating group.
- Whether the machine is producing ice, producing very little, or stopped.
- Whether the alarm followed a move, a water interruption, or other recent work.
- Any other display message visible from the normal user position.

Check only things you can observe safely from outside the machine. For example, note whether the normal water supply has been interrupted and whether stored items are obstructing accessible ventilation openings. Do not remove electrical covers, adjust control-board switches or open the refrigerant circuit.

## Why a reset is not the diagnosis

The linked service document describes a control-board alarm-reset procedure. It also states that servicing and maintenance are for qualified technicians. An internal button is not equivalent to an everyday user control.

A reset can remove the alarm state while leaving the underlying condition unchanged. If the machine alarms again, repeated clearing can make the original pattern harder to explain. Report the recurrence instead of assuming that each new alarm requires another reset.

## When to stop

Stop user-level troubleshooting when it would require entering a service compartment, testing live components, changing factory settings, or handling refrigeration components. A protective alarm deserves a model-specific assessment.

For a business using the ice machine, keep the operational issue and the handling of existing ice separate. An alarm explanation does not certify the quality or suitability of ice already in the bin. Follow your establishment’s procedures and the machine’s sanitation guidance.
