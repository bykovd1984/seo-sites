## How do I reset a La Crosse atomic clock?

First identify the model and decide whether it needs a restart, a time-zone correction or help receiving the WWVB radio signal. A factory restart may clear settings, and the procedure depends on the clock. La Crosse's published reset example describes an **analogue clock with hands, a SET button, a time-zone switch and one AA battery**; it should not be copied to an unrelated digital clock or weather station.

If the clock is exactly one hour wrong, check its time zone and daylight saving setting first. If it has stopped or the display is weak, check the specified battery and polarity. A reset cannot guarantee radio reception.

## Manufacturer reset example for an analogue atomic clock

La Crosse describes the following factory restart for the analogue configuration in its support article. Check that the controls and battery arrangement match your clock and consult model-specific instructions if they differ:

1. Remove the battery for 15 minutes.
2. Press SET at least 20 times while the battery is removed.
3. Select the appropriate time zone with the switch.
4. Insert one fresh AA LR6 1.5 V alkaline battery with the polarity shown.
5. Position the clock on an exterior wall facing Fort Collins, Colorado, as the manufacturer recommends for its example, and allow it time to receive the signal.

The manufacturer says the hands move to 12:00 during the search and may start running from that position if a signal is not received initially. Do not move the hands manually to correct them during synchronisation. Its guidance says reception and adjustment can take up to five nights.

The support page does not give a model number for this example. If your clock has different controls or battery requirements, do not assume these steps apply: identify the model and use its instructions.

## Why is the clock still wrong after a reset?

| What you see | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Correct minutes, wrong hour | Confirm the time zone and daylight saving setting for the model. |
| Hands at or moving from 12:00 after a restart | On the documented analogue example, this may be part of signal search; allow time for reception. |
| No signal after several nights | Check model-specific placement and signal-search instructions; building materials and location can affect reception. |
| Blank or faint digital display | Check the correct battery and orientation; digital clock reset steps can differ. |
| A weather station has lost an outdoor reading | This can be a sensor connection problem, not necessarily a clock reset. Use the station's own instructions. |

WWVB is a radio signal, so a clock can show a wrong local hour despite receiving the signal if the local time zone is set incorrectly. Daylight saving options and the locations they cover differ by model.

## Can I set the time manually instead?

Many models have a manual setting option, but controls differ. Our [La Crosse clock setting guide](/guides/la-crosse-clock-setting) gives a separate WT-8005 digital-clock example, including its initial signal search and time-zone setting. Do not use the analogue restart above on that model without confirming its instructions. If the clock still fails after the model-specific checks, contact La Crosse with its model number and describe the symptom.
