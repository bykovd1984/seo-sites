# Can you calibrate an OMRON blood pressure monitor at home?

If you own an OMRON M3 Comfort **HEM-7155-E** or X3 Comfort **HEM-7155-EO**, the accuracy check is a service job. OMRON directs owners to an authorized dealer or customer service and generally recommends inspection every two years. The owner controls have no pressure-calibration setting. If you are investigating a difference between home and clinic readings, keep the original numbers and take the monitor and its cuff to your appointment. [OMRON M3 and X3 Comfort instructions, section 5.4](https://www.omron-healthcare.co.uk/storage/im-hem-7155-eo-en-05-01-2022.pdf#page=8) · [American Heart Association home monitoring guidance](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home)

## When the home and office numbers differ

A reading taken at home before breakfast and another taken in the afternoon at the clinic give you two measurements under different conditions. You cannot calculate a correction to add to future readings from that pair. For your next routine reading, check that your upper arm falls within the size range printed on the cuff. Sit quietly for at least five minutes, put the cuff on bare skin, and rest your arm on a table with the cuff at heart level. Record the result and time. If the monitor flags movement or cuff fit, check the [warning symbol on your BP7250 or M3 Comfort](/guides/omron-symbols) before comparing readings. [American Heart Association instructions for measuring at home](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home)

## What to ask at a routine appointment

Bring the cuff you actually use, along with the monitor and a few dated readings. Ask a clinician to watch you take a measurement and compare your device with the office equipment. That gives them a chance to spot a poor fit or a change in arm position and to assess any difference that persists. The American Heart Association suggests this check when you first get a monitor and then about once a year, or as the manufacturer directs. [American Heart Association guidance on checking your monitor](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home)

## How to arrange an M3 or X3 inspection

Read the full model code from the unit. For UK owners, OMRON's M3 Comfort support page directs calibration questions to its contact form under **Servicing and Warranty Support**. Include the model and describe what prompted the request, such as a persistent difference found during an office comparison. The two-year recommendation in the M3/X3 instructions concerns an inspection of the instrument itself. For another model or country, use the service details supplied with that unit. [OMRON UK M3 Comfort support](https://www.omron-healthcare.co.uk/products/m3-comfort) · [OMRON UK contact form](https://www.omron-healthcare.co.uk/contact)

## What a factory reset does on the US BP7250

On this US 5 Series monitor, restoring defaults clears information stored in the unit. Its reset sequence offers no adjustment to the measured pressure. If the readings are the reason you want the monitor checked, keep them available for the comparison. [OMRON BP7250 instructions, section 6.2](https://omronhealthcare.com/storage/pdfs/5-series-wireless-upper-arm-blood-pressure-monitor-bp7250-im-en_3608778-6j.pdf#page=34)

## When a high reading needs urgent attention

For an adult who is not pregnant, a systolic reading over 180 mmHg or a diastolic reading over 120 mmHg with symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath or difficulty speaking is a medical emergency. Call local emergency services immediately. If you have no new or concerning symptoms, wait at least one minute and measure again. Contact your health care professional immediately if either number remains above its threshold. [American Heart Association guidance on very high blood pressure](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/when-to-call-911-for-high-blood-pressure)
