## Identify which light is flashing

The main work lamp, the small LED near a learn button, and the LEDs on the floor-level safety sensors are different indicators. Count the flashes from the relevant light and note the pause before the sequence repeats.

Write down the opener’s full model number. Craftsman branding spans different controls and generations. The examples here are useful only when the product’s own documentation identifies the corresponding Chamberlain-style reporting system.

## Separate the symptoms first

| Observation | A useful next check |
|---|---|
| Door opens but refuses to close | Whether the safety-sensor path is clear and the indicators match the model’s instructions |
| Door reverses while closing | The exact reversal behavior and any accompanying diagnostic pattern |
| Wall control works but remote does not | The remote-specific guidance and any lock indication on the wall control |
| No response from either control | Normal power indications and the model’s troubleshooting information |

The purpose of this split is to preserve the symptom, not diagnose a component from one sentence. Do not change travel or force settings before identifying the reason for a refusal to close.

## Ten work-light flashes on compatible older systems

Chamberlain Group describes ten main-light flashes with a refusal to close as a safety-sensor-related signal on supported older systems. Its support pages also say this particular reporting feature was removed from newer openers and replacement boards beginning in 2022.

Consequently, “no ten flashes” does not mean the sensors are healthy, and “ten flashes” is not a universal Craftsman code. Match the control system and documentation before using a chart.

Check for a visible obstruction across the sensor path. Observe whether the sensor LEDs are steady, flickering or off, then compare that observation with the correct guide. Do not bridge the sensors or defeat their purpose to make the door move.

## A learn-button LED is a different chart

Some older systems use repeated flashes from the LED beside the learn button to report faults. That count is not the same as the work-lamp count. Photograph the control layout and record the small-LED pattern separately.

Do not assume that a table for one board applies to a newer unit with separate up/down indicators. Keep the light location, pattern and model together when contacting support.

## What not to reset or adjust speculatively

Erasing learned controls can remove remote access without fixing a closing fault. Changing force or travel can introduce a different problem. A reset is a procedure for a defined situation, not a diagnosis.

Leave spring tension, cables, door balance repairs, live electrical work and internal board work to an appropriate professional. If the door is crooked, a cable is displaced, or there is visible mechanical damage, keep people clear and seek service rather than continuing movement tests.

Prepare a short report: model, which controls work, where movement stops, and which light repeats which count. That gives the service provider an actionable starting point.
