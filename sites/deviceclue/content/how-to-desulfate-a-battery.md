## How do you desulfate a battery?

For a **compatible lead-acid battery**, some manufacturer-approved smart chargers include a desulfation stage. Identify the battery chemistry, voltage and condition first, then use a charger and charging programme explicitly approved for that battery. Follow both manufacturers’ instructions. Desulfation is an attempt at recovery, not a guarantee that capacity or reliability will return.

Do not apply lead-acid desulfation instructions to a lithium battery. Do not charge a battery that is leaking, damaged, frozen or abnormally hot; stop and seek appropriate professional advice. Charging lead-acid batteries can produce explosive gas, so follow the equipment’s ventilation, connection and ignition-source precautions.

## Decide whether recovery is appropriate

1. **Read the battery label.** Establish chemistry, voltage and any charging limits. AGM, gel and flooded batteries can have different requirements; do not assume a recovery programme is suitable for all three.
2. **Inspect without opening the case.** Damage, leakage, freezing, swelling or unusual heat are reasons to stop rather than start a recovery attempt.
3. **Match the charger and programme.** Check both the battery maker’s limits and the exact charger instructions. A charger that supports lead-acid batteries does not necessarily permit every optional high-voltage or reconditioning mode on every battery.
4. **Follow the documented connection and charging procedure.** Use the specified ventilation and protective measures. Do not improvise a power supply, add household chemicals or bypass charger protections.
5. **Evaluate the result.** A completed charging stage or a higher voltage is not a capacity test. If performance matters, arrange the appropriate battery condition or load test and replace a battery that fails it.

Our [guide to sulfated battery meaning](/guides/sulfated-battery-meaning) explains how persistent sulfate deposits differ from an ordinary discharged battery and why external terminal deposits cannot diagnose the plates.

## How long does desulfation take?

There is **no universal time**. It depends on the charger, its programme and the battery’s condition. As one bounded example, the CTEK PRO25S North American manual lists a maximum of eight hours for its first desulfation stage. That is a stage limit for that charger’s documented lead-acid programme, not the total charging time, a required duration for another device, or evidence of a successful repair.

The same guide describes subsequent charging and analysis stages and a separate optional RECOND stage. A desulfation stage and a reconditioning setting are not interchangeable terms or universally appropriate settings. Follow your exact charger and battery documentation; if the charger reports a fault or exceeds its documented behaviour, stop and seek support.

## Does a SUL message mean the battery is fixed?

No. A message is the charger’s indication, not a measurement proving usable capacity. The meaning and response depend on the charger model. Our [SUL charger message guide](/guides/sul-on-battery-charger) covers one documented example and its limitations.

## When should you replace the battery instead?

Do not keep repeating recovery cycles to make a damaged battery appear usable. If a suitable charger cannot complete its process, the battery fails an appropriate test, or it cannot reliably meet its intended load, get a professional assessment and consider replacement. Recycle the old battery through an appropriate collection programme.
