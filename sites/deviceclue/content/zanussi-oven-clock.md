## First, identify the control panel

Look for the exact model and Product Number Code (PNC) on the appliance data plate, normally accessible around the oven frame with the door open. Take a photo of the panel as well. The brand name alone does not tell you which programmer is fitted.

Zanussi’s support page shows several different layouts. Some have plus and minus buttons; others use a rotary control. Match the picture and the instructions before choosing a sequence. The schematic on this page shows an idea, not your actual panel.

If the appliance is a Smeg rather than a Zanussi, use the separate [Smeg oven clock guide](/guides/smeg-oven-clock). Similar-looking buttons do not make the clock-setting sequences interchangeable.

## Set the time on a matching +/− panel

For the +/− panels illustrated in Zanussi’s support article, the time indicator flashes after the appliance is connected or its clock loses power. Use the plus or minus control to adjust the displayed time, then stop pressing buttons. The documented examples save the setting after about five seconds.

When changing a time that is already set, select the time-of-day function first using the panel’s function control. Check its symbol in the matching instructions: a minute minder or cooking-duration setting is a different function.

- Set the time while the oven is not being used for cooking.
- Confirm that you are changing the time of day, not entering a countdown.
- Wait for the flashing to stop and check the saved display.
- If the controls do not match the manufacturer’s example, stop and use the instructions selected by PNC.

## What if the oven has a rotary control?

Do not apply the +/− sequence to a panel that does not have those controls. Zanussi documents a different interface in which the temperature knob changes hours and minutes, with confirmation between them. The page also shows the confirmation symbol. Use the image in that source and your model documentation rather than guessing a nearby button.

For an Electrolux-branded appliance, see our [Electrolux oven clock guide](/guides/electrolux-oven-clock) and match its examples to that panel.

The useful distinction is the control type: the same task can have a different entry point, confirmation step and saving behavior.

## Flashing after a power cut

A flashing 00:00 or 12:00 can follow a power interruption or a new installation. Setting the clock again may be all that is needed. It does not, by itself, establish a failed clock module or heating element.

If the display loses its setting again, record when it happens and whether other appliances also lost power. That is useful information for a service enquiry. Repeatedly changing timer settings will not tell you why power or a saved setting was lost.

## Clock, timer or heating problem?

| What you see | What to identify next |
|---|---|
| Flashing time after the power returned | The time-of-day setting for the fitted panel |
| A countdown that decreases | Whether the minute minder was selected |
| Correct time but no heat | The separate no-heating checks for the exact model |
| Buttons do not respond to the documented steps | Panel identity, any displayed lock indicator, and service guidance |

Do not open the control panel or expose wiring to fix a clock setting. If the correct user-level instructions do not work, give the service provider the model, PNC and a clear description of the display.

For a cooking-function question, see our [Zanussi oven symbols guide](/guides/zanussi-oven-symbols); a clock setting and a heating mode serve different purposes.
