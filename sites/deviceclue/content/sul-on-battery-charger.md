## Read the message in the correct document

The Schumacher SC1325 product page links a charger document whose display-message table includes SUL. In that table, the charger has detected a sulfated battery and enters a desulfation process.

There is an important edition limit: the linked document is labelled 101-11 / 101-12. Check the charger model, document revision and battery requirements before applying the table to your equipment. A manufacturer link is useful evidence, but it does not prove compatibility with every unit sold under a product name.

## SUL is not the same as a successful repair

The message describes what the charger is attempting. It does not establish the battery’s remaining capacity or prove that it will perform normally afterward. A status change on the charger and a battery-condition assessment answer different questions.

For the battery term itself, see [what a sulfated battery means](/guides/sulfated-battery-meaning). This article focuses on the message and the next information to check.

## Keep different codes separate

| Message in the linked document | Meaning described there | Limit to remember |
|---|---|---|
| SUL | Sulfation detected; desulfation process begins | It is not a confirmation of recovery |
| F02 | The charger cannot desulfate the battery | Follow the document’s check-or-replace guidance |
| Another code | A different condition in the model’s table | Do not infer its meaning from SUL |

Do not paste a generic online list of F-codes onto a different charger. Some definitions depend on battery voltage, elapsed time and the charging stage. Leaving those conditions out can change the meaning.

## What to check before doing anything else

- Record the charger’s full model and the exact display text.
- Read the battery label for its chemistry and rated voltage.
- Confirm that both the battery and the selected charging mode are supported.
- Use the appropriate document edition to interpret the status and eventual result.

Do not assume that a lead-acid recovery mode is suitable for a lithium battery, or that a setting for one lead-acid type is appropriate for another. Compatibility is a prerequisite, not something a successful connection proves.

## Should you restart it until SUL disappears?

Repeatedly restarting a charger is not evidence that the battery is improving. Follow the supported operating procedure and its defined result instead of trying to suppress the display message. A recurring failure deserves an assessment of the battery and the charging setup.

Do not attempt to charge a frozen, leaking, visibly damaged or abnormally hot battery. Keep ignition sources away and follow the manufacturer’s ventilation, connection and disconnection precautions. This explanation does not replace the complete safety instructions or provide a forced-recovery procedure.

If the result remains uncertain, give a suitable battery-service provider the model, battery label, message sequence and final charger result. Ask for an appropriate condition assessment rather than a promise that desulfation will restore the original battery.
